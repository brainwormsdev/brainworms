Real

The wiring

The published whole-body connectome of a three-day-old Platynereis dumerilii larva: 2,675 cells and cell fragments, 14,066 connections, 26,881 synapses, each traced by hand from serial electron micrographs.

Every connection and synapse count is taken straight from that dataset. Nothing is added or rewired.

1,199 cells sit at a soma position from the lab's published 3D reconstructions of their cell type, on the correct body side. 1,009 more have no published position and are placed in their correct body segment and side. 467 fragments are simulated but not drawn.

Simplified

The model

Each cell is a simple firing-rate unit. Its input is the activity of the cells that synapse onto it, weighted by synapse count and divided by its total incoming synapses.

  • Which transmitter each cell uses is unknown for most of them, so every synapse is treated as excitatory.
  • One global gain (2.2) and slow per-cell fatigue keep activity from running away.
  • Nothing is trained. There are no per-synapse weights beyond the published counts.

The worm can't read. Your message reaches it only as light and dark moving across its eyes.

Readouts

What you see

  • One worm: the server runs the only simulation, 30 steps a second, and streams its activity to everyone. Messages play one at a time, in order.
  • Eyes: the 13 left photoreceptors each sample a strip of the left half of the view, and the 13 right ones the right half. The strip layout is our choice; the real receptive fields aren't in the data.
  • Bright views: the brain's 4 non-directional light sensors only get input when most of the view is bright. In this model they set off the whole body.
  • Poke: activates the touch sensors (collar receptors and chaeta mechanosensors) nearest your tap.
  • Meters: left minus right activity of the longitudinal muscles and ciliated swimming cells, and the chaetal and parapodial muscles.
  • Check it: every message and poke is written to a public event log. Re-running it with the open-source code reproduces every summary in the feed.
